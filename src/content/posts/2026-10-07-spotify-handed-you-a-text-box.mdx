Sarah Perez, for [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/23/spotify-is-giving-you-the-keys-to-its-recommendation-algorithm-with-u-s-launch-of-taste-profile/):

Spotify is rolling out Taste Profile to Premium subscribers in the U.S., 18 and up. You open your profile, read how Spotify describes your taste across music, podcasts, and audiobooks, and type what you want changed into a box labeled "Tell us more." TechCrunch's framing is generous:

> In some ways, Taste Profile essentially gives you the keys to Spotify's algorithm.

Keys would mean you get to drive. What you get is Spotify's summary of you, a text field, and a wait. Per TechCrunch, your Home page reflects a different set of suggestions "after a few hours." You do not edit the model. You file a request with it and check back later.

The feature is also an admission. Spotify's taste modeling is usually credited as the reason it won, yet TechCrunch notes it often recommends too-similar music when a listener wants to branch out, and gets pulled around by sleep sounds at night or whatever a teenager plays in the car on a parent's account. Until now the fixes were excluding specific tracks or playlists, plus a toggle to keep kids' music out. A plain-language complaint form is what you build when the black box is wrong often enough that the toggles stopped covering it.

It is still a beta, still Premium only, and still Spotify's own AI deciding what your request means. Spotify showed it at SXSW in March, tested it in New Zealand, and gave no timeline for a wider rollout.

That is a better feedback channel. It is not ownership. The profile that drives Discover Weekly, Made for You, and Wrapped still belongs to the company that wrote it.
