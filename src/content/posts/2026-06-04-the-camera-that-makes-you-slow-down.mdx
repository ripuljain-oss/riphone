The Fujifilm X100VI is the best camera for anyone who stopped carrying a camera because their phone was close enough. The phone is not close enough, but that's the wrong argument to make about the X100VI.

The VI adds in-body stabilization to the compact that already had everything worth having: a 40MP sensor, a fixed 23mm f/2 lens, a leaf shutter that fires silently in places where silence matters, a built-in ND filter, and film simulations that have nothing to do with Instagram presets. The stabilization changes street shooting in low light. A stationary subject and a shaky hand used to ruin the frame. Now it usually doesn't.

Your iPhone takes technically better photos in a parking garage at midnight. That's not the point.

The X100VI is about intention. Fixed focal length means you move your feet. The EVF means you put the camera to your eye instead of holding it at arm's length. The aperture ring and shutter dial mean you made a decision before the shutter fired. You come home with thirty frames instead of three hundred, and most of the thirty are worth keeping.

The film simulations are the other argument. Nostalgic Neg, Classic Chrome, Eterna Bleach Bypass. They're not filters. They're Fujifilm's opinion about how color should behave under different conditions. You learn to match simulation to subject, which is a different kind of thinking than correcting in Lightroom after the fact.

The obvious alternative is the Ricoh GR IIIx. Smaller, cheaper, and the 28mm-equivalent lens is sharper at the edges. No viewfinder, which for some people is fine. For me the EVF is what makes the X100VI a camera and not a fancy point-and-shoot. If you don't use viewfinders, buy the Ricoh.

At around $1,600, the X100VI is not an impulse purchase. It's not a second camera for someone who already shoots seriously with a mirrorless kit. It's a first camera for someone who wants to shoot more carefully, or a travel camera for someone whose main system is too much to carry through a city for six hours.

If you shoot raw and want to control everything in post, the X100VI will frustrate you. The fixed lens will frustrate you. Buy a zoom. If you want to take photography seriously without carrying serious gear, this is the most honest compact camera made.

It is small enough to have with you. Jacket pocket, travel bag, no lens swap, no bag of gear. The best camera is the one that shows up. Mine is linked on the <a href="/gear" class="link">gear page</a>.
