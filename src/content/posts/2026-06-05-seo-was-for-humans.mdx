Mark Tyson, reporting for Tom's Hardware on Cloudflare's latest traffic data:

> Welp, that happened faster than I predicted. Thought it would be end of 2027, then early 2027, but agentic traffic growing so fast that bots have now passed human traffic online for the first time in the Internet's history.

The split is 57.5% bot requests to 42.5% human — and this isn't the old crawler-and-scraper traffic. These are agentic bots: price-checkers, comparison shoppers, research assistants, the things fetching pages on your behalf before you ever open a browser tab.

Here's what nobody in the SEO conversation wants to say out loud: we've been optimizing for the wrong customer.

For twenty years, SEO meant ranking in Google so a human would click. Title tags, meta descriptions, keyword density, backlink farms dressed up as content strategy. The entire discipline assumed a person would land on your page, scan it, and maybe convert. That person was the audience.

The audience is changing. Not disappearing — Tom's Hardware notes humans still dominate time spent in apps and feeds. But HTTP requests, the thing that actually hits your server and burns your bandwidth, are now mostly machines. Your page gets read by an agent first. The human may never visit at all — they'll get a summary, a comparison table, a recommendation synthesized from what the agent scraped.

That's a different product problem. As a PM, I think about who the user is and what job they're hiring the page to do. The job used to be "convince a human to trust me enough to click." Now it's increasingly "give a machine-parseable answer an agent can cite correctly." Those are not the same spec.

Structured data. Clean semantic HTML. Explicit authorship and dates. Machine-readable site guides. Facts stated plainly, not buried under engagement bait. This is what agents need — and it's what most SEO-optimized content is terrible at, because it was written to game a ranking algorithm, not to be understood.

I run `/llms.txt` on this site for exactly that reason. Not because I'm prescient — because I build with AI tools daily and I know how agents actually consume the web. They don't care about your hero image. They want to know what you said, when you said it, and whether you're worth citing.

The SEO industry will rebrand this as "AEO" or "GEO" and sell you a consulting retainer. Ignore the acronym. The shift is simpler: your page's first reader is probably not a person anymore. Write for that.
