The Moccamaster makes the same cup I was making with a V60, a gooseneck kettle, and eight minutes of my morning. It takes about six.

The [KBG Select](/gear)'s copper heating element hits 200 degrees and holds it. The showerhead saturates the grounds evenly across the basket. Together they do the work the pourover ritual used to require of me. The bloom, the concentric pours, the careful drawdown: all of that was me approximating what the machine just does automatically.

Pour-over has a ritual appeal that's real. There are mornings when the process is the point. But there are more mornings when I just want coffee, and the Moccamaster is never an obstacle on those days. You grind, fill the reservoir, press a button. Six minutes later there's a full carafe that tastes like someone who was paying attention made it.

The tradeoff is control. A V60 adjusts to the beans, the grind, the mood. The Moccamaster has one speed: hot and even. Within that constraint it's excellent. Outside it, you still need the manual setup.

The closest competitor in the same category is the Breville Precision Brewer. Also SCAA-certified, also hitting the right brew temperature, and about $100 less. The Breville has adjustable bloom time, water flow rate, and a pre-infusion mode. If you want to tune your drip variables, buy the Breville. The Moccamaster has one mode and it's correct. That's either a feature or a limitation depending on your relationship with coffee.

The build changes the price calculation. Technivorm has been making this machine in the Netherlands since 1969. The heating element is copper, the housing is aluminum and polycarbonate. It looks like it will outlast everything around it. At $350 amortized over a decade, that's $35 a year.

Who should skip it: anyone who drinks one cup and doesn't want a carafe sitting around, anyone looking for espresso-adjacent results, anyone who considers the manual ritual the point rather than the obstacle. The Moccamaster does nothing interesting. That's the pitch.

At around $350, it's not cheap for a drip brewer. It's very cheap for something that removes the variable of whether you had enough patience that morning.
