Snap unveiled Specs this week, its first AR glasses aimed at the public rather than developers, at $2,195 and shipping this fall. This is the fourth time I have watched Snap strap a camera to your face and call it the future. The first three were developer kits that went nowhere. What is different now is not the hardware. It is the pitch.

Evan Spiegel, speaking to [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2195862/snap-specs-ceo-evan-spiegel-interview-at-awe-2026/) at AWE 2026:

> I think AI glasses are typically being used to record content, that's sort of the purpose of the glasses as they're marketed. That's not the purpose of Specs.

He is drawing a circle around Meta without saying the name. Ray-Bans record. Specs compute. He calls Specs "a new type of computer, a see-through computer," and insists it is not AI glasses. This is a man fighting over what to call the category, which is exactly what you do when you cannot win it on volume.

And he cannot. Meta is selling camera glasses by the millions with no display at all. Google has Android XR and Samsung behind it. Apple has the patience and the balance sheet to wait everyone out. Snap has a soft ad business, a stock that has spent years underwater, and roughly none of the cash any of those companies will pour into this. The one thing Snap genuinely owns is the best small-form optical AR anyone has shown and a decade of developer lenses to run on it. That part is real. It is also not enough to win a hardware platform war.

So Specs is not really a consumer product. It is a positioning move. If glasses ever break out, Snap wants to be the operating system, not an app you install on someone else's frames. Owning the phrase "see-through computer" is the first land grab. Keeping the developers and the AR talent is the second. Looking like the obvious thing to partner with or buy is the third.

Watch the pricing and you can see Snap knows it. Spiegel says he wants Specs to be "as accessible as possible," then prices it at $2,195. Accessible to whom? Developers and early adopters, the same audience as the last three kits he swore were different.

Snap has the best AR glasses anyone has put on a face. It is also the company least able to afford selling them. Specs exists to keep that contradiction from resolving the wrong way.
