Every notes app I have ever used eventually turned into a second job. The capture is free. The filing is the tax. You jot something down at a stoplight, and then you owe the app a folder, a tag, a title, a tidy little home for a thought that arrived half-formed. Most of my notes died in that gap. Not because the idea was bad, but because I never paid the tax.

So I built [Musing](https://musingnotes.app). It is on the App Store today, iPhone only, and the whole premise is that you never organize anything.

You capture the mess. Text mid-sentence, a voice memo while you are walking, a photo of a whiteboard. It keeps the original audio, reads the text in your photos, quietly records where and when you were. Then later you ask for it back in plain language. "That thing I said about pricing on the train last week." Claude does the finding. There are no folders to maintain, because there are no folders.

I want to be honest about the skeptic's objection, because it is mine too. AI search in a notes app is the kind of feature every company is bolting on right now to look current. Most of it is theater. A semantic search box that returns the same three notes you could have found by scrolling. The difference is whether the retrieval is good enough that you stop organizing entirely, or just good enough for the demo. I have been using this daily for months, and the test I held it to was simple: do I trust it enough to throw things in and forget them. I do now. That took longer to get right than I expected.

The other thing I refused to compromise on is the part nobody markets well. Notes live on your device, backed up through your own iCloud. Nothing is sold. Nothing trains a model. I did not build a funnel disguised as a notebook. The AI runs on your stuff to serve you, and that is the end of the arrangement.

Pricing is boring on purpose, and there is no subscription. Capture is unlimited and free forever, because charging people to write down a thought is hostile. The only thing you ever pay for is AI search, and only if you want more of it. You buy credits once, they never expire, and nothing recurs. Most people will not hit the free monthly limit anyway.

I write here mostly to call out other people's products. It is fair that you get to do the same to mine. [It is on the App Store.](https://musingnotes.app)
