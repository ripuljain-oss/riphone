Anthropic, on redeploying Claude Fable 5:

The most capable AI model ever sold disappeared for 19 days, and every company that built on it just learned where the kill switch lives. Not in a datacenter. In the Commerce Department.

> This required us to restrict access to foreign nationals, whether inside or outside the United States. Because the order took effect immediately and we had no reliable way to verify nationality in real-time, we suspended access to both models for all users.

The timeline is worth sitting with. Fable 5 launched June 9. Three days later, after Amazon researchers reported a way around its safeguards for finding software vulnerabilities, the government applied export controls. Anthropic had no way to check passports at the API, so the model went dark for everyone, everywhere.

Anthropic's own post quietly undercuts the whole premise. Its testing confirmed that “many less capable models—including Claude Opus 4.8, GPT-5.5, and Kimi K2.7—could identify the same vulnerabilities.” The takedown protected nothing you couldn't already do with the models that stayed up.

Now look at what it took to turn it back on. Not a court ruling. A new classifier that blocks the reported technique, plus [an agreement](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/7/1/us-lifts-restrictions-on-powerful-ai-models-fable-mythos-anthropic-says) that Anthropic will proactively detect security risks, coordinate with the government on standards, and report malicious activity. That's not a reversal. That's a settlement. The export control didn't fail, it converted into a standing arrangement nobody had to legislate.

And the collateral damage ran the wrong direction. During the blackout, [Chinese models like GLM-5.2 and DeepSeek-V4 gained traction](https://www.forbes.com/sites/ronschmelzer/2026/06/29/is-anthropics-fable-5-coming-back-this-week/) with developers who needed something that couldn't be switched off by a Commerce order. A policy built to keep capability out of the wrong hands spent three weeks marketing the alternatives.

I ship this blog through Claude Code, so I lived the outage as a user, not a spectator. The model is back, cheaper than the preview and better than anything else I've used. But the lesson of June isn't that the system worked. It's that the switch is real, it's been thrown once, and the price of turning it back on was moving it inside the building.
